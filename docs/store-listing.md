### Full description (2,204 of 4,000)

```
OpenRoutine helps you start your routine and stay with it, one step at a time.

Build a routine out of small steps, give each step a time, and let the timer walk you through. When a step's time is up, you get a gentle nudge. No guilt, no nagging, no lost progress if you need a minute.

Meet Ochi, a small friendly mascot who cheers you on and throws a little party when you finish.

WHAT YOU CAN DO
• Build routines from steps, with emojis, durations, and optional days and start times.
• Start from ready-made step templates for morning, evening, study, and self-care.
• Follow a routine with Timer Mode: a visual countdown for the current step, with pause and skip.
• Get a gentle reminder when a step's estimate ends, and a heads-up before a scheduled routine begins.
• Use Low Mode on lower-capacity days. Mark the steps that really matter, then run only those. Finishing a Low Mode routine counts.
• See your streaks and history at a glance.
• Add the Android home screen widget to start a routine in one tap.
• Pick your accent color and light, dark, or system theme.
• Use it in English or Spanish.

YOUR DATA STAYS YOURS
• No account. No sign-up. There's no OpenRoutine server.
• Works fully offline. Your routines are stored on your phone.
• Optional backup and sync to your own Google Drive. OpenRoutine asks for Google's narrowest Drive permission, so it can only see the folder it creates, never your other files.
• Your routines are saved as plain JSON files you can read, copy, or share. If you want, an AI assistant that can read your Drive can help you review or plan routines. OpenRoutine itself never talks to an AI service.
• No ads. No analytics. No tracking.

OPEN SOURCE
OpenRoutine is free and open source under the MIT license. There's no Pro tier and no feature gating. Read the code, report an issue, or contribute at github.com/asloth/openroutine.

A NOTE ON REMINDERS
The timer on screen is exact. When your phone is locked, it still tells you when a step ends, though Android may deliver the notification a few minutes late to save battery. Notifications are scheduled on your phone, and the app asks for permission when you start your first timer.

Built for people who find starting hard, and who deserve a routine that bends on the hard days.
```

## Play Data safety

Answers for the Data safety form in Play Console. They match [`privacy-policy.md`](privacy-policy.md). I checked the app's code: the only network traffic is to Google's Drive API (`www.googleapis.com`), and there's no analytics, crash reporting, advertising, or Firebase SDK.

| Question | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Does your app collect or share any of the required user data types? | **No** | The developer never receives user data. Drive sync goes from the phone to the user's own Drive, and Play's definition of "collect" means data sent off the device to you or a third party you work with. |
| Is all of the user data collected by your app encrypted in transit? | **Yes** | Drive calls use HTTPS. Answer this even though nothing's collected, if the console asks. |
| Do you provide a way for users to request that their data be deleted? | **Yes** | Users delete data in the app, uninstall it, or delete the `OpenRoutine` folder in their Drive. See the policy's "Deleting your data" section. |
| Privacy policy URL | `https://asloth.github.io/openroutine/privacy/` | Required. Published by `.github/workflows/pages.yml`. |

Data types, one by one. All are **not collected** and **not shared**:

- **Location, personal info, financial info, health and fitness, messages, photos and videos, audio files, contacts, calendar, web browsing, app info and performance, device or other IDs:** not collected. The app doesn't read any of them. The random install ID stays on the phone and in the user's own Drive.
- **Files and docs:** the app reads and writes only files it creates in the user's Drive, and a file the user picks for import. Not collected by the developer.
- **App activity:** routine and completion history stay on the device and in the user's Drive. Not collected by the developer.

This is a judgment call: Drive sync sends data off the device, but only to the user's own Drive, at their request, with the developer never receiving it. Play's form has no "user's own storage" option, so "No" is the honest reading. Confirm it against the current Data safety help text before you submit, and keep the policy and the in-app Drive explanation in step with it.

**Sensitive-scope note:** `drive.file` isn't a restricted scope, so you don't need the CASA security assessment. You do need OAuth consent-screen verification. See the publishing checklist.

## Permissions declaration

Android permissions that end up in the release build (the manifest file plus what plugins merge in). Items that need an explanation in Play Console come first.

| Permission | Where it comes from | Plain-language reason for the form | Play treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` | App manifest | Tells you when a step's time is up and reminds you before a scheduled routine. The app asks when you first start a timer. | Runtime permission. No declaration. |
| `RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED` | App manifest | Re-schedules pending alarms and reminders after the phone restarts or the app updates, so your reminders don't silently vanish. | Normal. No declaration. |
| `INTERNET` | Merged from plugins | Talks to Google's Drive API when you connect Google Drive. | Normal. No declaration. |
| `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE` | Merged from plugins | Lets the Google sign-in and networking libraries check whether you're online. | Normal. |
| `VIBRATE` | Merged from plugins | Vibrates with step reminders. | Normal. |
| `WAKE_LOCK` | Merged from plugins | Lets the system wake the app briefly to post a scheduled notification. | Normal. |
| `FOREGROUND_SERVICE` | Merged from plugins | Declared by a library the app uses. The app doesn't start a foreground service of its own. | Normal. If Play asks, say the app uses no foreground service types. |

What the app doesn't request: exact alarms (`USE_EXACT_ALARM` or `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM`, see [`SPEC.md`](SPEC.md) §15.14), location, contacts, camera, microphone, photos, files and media, SMS, phone, accessibility, or "all files access".

### Text for the notification permission

> OpenRoutine uses notifications to tell the user when a timed step in their routine has finished and to remind them before a scheduled routine starts. The app asks for notification permission when the user starts their first timer.
